Numbers on radio: 0 "ZE-RO", 3 "TREE", 4 "FOW-er", 5 "FIFE", 9 "NIN-er". Common terms: "Over" = I'm done, your turn. "Out" = conversation finished. "Roger" = received. "Wilco" = will comply. "Say again" = repeat. "Mayday" (x3) = life-threatening emergency (voice equivalent of SOS).

NOAA Weather Radio (NWR) Frequencies

NOAA broadcasts continuous weather and emergency alerts 24/7 on these 7 VHF channels. A weather radio (or scanner) receives them. No license needed to listen.

ChannelFrequency (MHz)

WX2162.425

Tune through all 7 and stay on the strongest one for your area. Listen for the 1050 Hz alert tone before warnings.

Common simplex prep frequency people monitor: FRS/GMRS Ch 3 and the "2-mile" 462.6125. CB radio Channel 9 (27.065 MHz) is the traditional emergency channel; Channel 19 (27.185) is the highway/travel channel.

In a real emergency: transmit slowly and clearly. State WHO you are, WHERE you are, WHAT you need. Repeat. Listen between transmissions. Conserve battery — short messages, agreed check-in times (for example, on the hour).
