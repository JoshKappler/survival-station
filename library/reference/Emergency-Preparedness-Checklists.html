Emergency Preparedness Checklists

Plain-language checklists compiled from public emergency-preparedness guidance (Ready.gov / FEMA / CDC themes). For the full in-depth guide, open FEMA-Are-You-Ready.pdf in this same folder. Print or copy what matters to you.

The four basics of being ready: 1) Have water and food. 2) Have a way to get information (battery/solar radio). 3) Have first-aid and medicine. 4) Have a plan for where to go and how your household will reconnect.

Basic Emergency Supply Kit

Water & Food

  • Water: ~1 gallon (4 L) per person per day, at least 3 days (2 weeks if you can)
  • Way to purify water (bleach, filter, or pot to boil)
  • Non-perishable food, several days’ worth
  • Manual can opener
  • Cooking method that needs no grid power

Light, Power & Info

  • Flashlight(s) and spare batteries
  • Battery or hand-crank / solar radio (NOAA weather band)
  • Extra batteries; power bank; solar charger
  • Whistle to signal for help
  • Matches/lighter in a waterproof container

Health & Hygiene

  • First-aid kit and the first-aid book (see medical folder)
  • 7+ day supply of prescription medicines
  • Pain reliever, anti-diarrhea, antihistamine, antiseptic
  • Soap, hand sanitizer, toilet paper, garbage bags
  • Moist towelettes; feminine and personal items
  • N95/dust masks

Tools, Warmth & Documents

  • Multi-tool or knife; duct tape; work gloves
  • Wrench/pliers to shut off utilities
  • Blankets / sleeping bags; warm clothes; rain gear
  • Local maps (paper)
  • Copies of IDs, insurance, medical info in a waterproof bag
  • Some cash in small bills

Make a Household Plan

  • Meeting places: agree on one spot right outside your home, and one outside your neighborhood if you can’t get home.
  • Out-of-area contact: pick one relative/friend far away everyone can check in with; it’s often easier to reach a distant contact than a local one.
  • Write it down: names, phone numbers, addresses, medical needs, on paper in each person’s bag.
  • Know how to shut off your water, gas, and electricity, and keep the tools to do it.
  • Plan for everyone: children, older adults, anyone with medical needs, and pets (food, water, carrier, records).

If You Must Evacuate

  • Leave early; don’t wait until roads are blocked.
  • Take your "go-bag," medicines, documents, phone + charger, water, and warm clothing.
  • Turn off utilities only if instructed or if you smell gas / see damage.
  • Tell your out-of-area contact where you are going.
  • Follow official routes; avoid flooded roads — "Turn Around, Don’t Drown."

If You Shelter in Place

  • Stay informed with the weather/emergency radio.
  • Keep warm without the grid: layer clothing, close off one room, never run a generator, grill, or fuel heater indoors (carbon-monoxide kills).
  • Conserve phone and radio battery; agreed check-in times save power.
  • Ration water; treat any questionable water before drinking.
  • Keep the fridge/freezer closed to hold cold as long as possible.

Water: Store & Make Safe

  • Store: commercially bottled water is best; or fill clean food-grade containers and rotate every 6 months.
  • Boil: rolling boil 1 minute (3 minutes above ~6,500 ft) — the surest method.
  • Bleach: 2 drops of unscented 5–6% bleach per liter/quart of clear water (8 drops/gallon); wait 30 min; faint chlorine smell = OK.
  • If water is cloudy, filter through a clean cloth first, then disinfect; double the bleach.

Hazard-Specific Quick Notes

  • Fire: get out, stay out, crawl low under smoke; have two ways out of each room.
  • Earthquake: Drop, Cover, Hold On; stay away from windows; expect aftershocks.
  • Flood: move to higher ground; never walk/drive through moving water.
  • Tornado: go to a small interior room on the lowest floor; cover your head.
  • Extreme heat: drink water, rest in shade/cool, watch for dizziness/confusion (heat stroke is an emergency).
  • Extreme cold: layer up, stay dry, watch for shivering that stops, slurred speech, drowsiness (hypothermia).
  • Power outage: keep freezers closed; never use outdoor fuel equipment indoors; disconnect sensitive electronics.
Stay calm. Most emergencies are survived by people who prepared a little and kept their heads. Check on neighbors, especially the elderly and those alone. Use the manuals in this library — they were put here for exactly this moment.
