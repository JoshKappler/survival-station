Signaling & SOS Reference

How to call for help and be found when you have no working phone. The rule of rescue signals: threes mean distress, and contrast + movement get you seen.

The Distress Signal: SOS

···   −−−   ···

Three short, three long, three short — sent as one group, then pause, then repeat. Works as sound (whistle, banging), light (flashlight, mirror, signal fire), or radio. By voice on a radio, say "MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY" for a life-threatening emergency.

Rule of Three (universal distress)

MethodDistress signal
Whistle blasts3 blasts, repeated
Fires / smoke columns3 fires in a triangle or a straight line
Flashlight / mirror flashes3 flashes, pause, repeat
Gunshots3 evenly spaced shots

A whistle carries far and costs almost no energy — far better than shouting. Three of almost anything, evenly spaced, reads as "help."

Signal Mirror

On a sunny day a mirror flash can be seen for many miles — one of the most effective signals there is. Hold the mirror near your eye, stretch your other hand toward the aircraft/rescuer, and flash the bright spot across your fingers onto the target. Sweep the horizon even if you see no one.

Ground-to-Air Signals

Make these LARGE (the bigger the better, at least 3 m / 10 ft) using rocks, logs, branches, tramped snow, or clothing — anything that contrasts with the ground. Place in a clearing.

Making Yourself Visible

  • Contrast: bright orange, red, or anything unnatural against the background; in snow, use dark objects.
  • Movement: wave a flag or bright cloth — the eye catches motion.
  • Smoke: by day, add green leaves/damp wood for white smoke; oil, rubber, or plastic for black smoke. Choose the color that contrasts with the sky/ground.
  • Light at night: a fire is visible for miles; flash a light toward any sound of engines.
  • Stay put if you are lost and someone may be looking — a stationary, visible person is far easier to find. Make your signals where you can be seen from the air.
Priorities if injured and alone: first stop life-threatening bleeding and protect yourself from cold/heat, THEN signal. A clear, well-placed signal you can maintain beats exhausting yourself. See the survival and first-aid manuals in this library.
