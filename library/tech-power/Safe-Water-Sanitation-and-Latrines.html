Safe Water, Sanitation & Latrines

Clean water and safe disposal of human waste prevent most deadly outbreaks (cholera, dysentery, typhoid) after a disaster. This is plain-language guidance based on public WHO and CDC recommendations. For depth, see the water-supply and hygiene manuals in this library.

The single most important rule: keep human waste AWAY from drinking water and food. Most emergency illness and death comes from the cycle: feces → hands/flies/water → mouth. Break that cycle and you save lives.

Making Water Safe to Drink

If you are not certain water is safe, treat it. Cloudy water: let it settle and/or filter through a clean cloth FIRST, then disinfect.

For the safest result, filter cloudy water, then boil or chlorinate. Store treated water in a clean, covered container and dip it out with a clean ladle — never with hands.

Protecting Your Water Source

  • Collect from the cleanest source: protected spring, rainwater, deep well — not surface water near waste.
  • Keep latrines, animals, and washing at least 30 meters (100 ft) away from and DOWNHILL of any well or spring.
  • Cover wells and water containers. Keep buckets and ropes off the ground.
  • Disinfect storage containers periodically with a little bleach solution.

Well Water Basics

  • A safe well is cased (lined), has a sealed cover, and sits on higher ground than any latrine or animal pen.
  • After flooding or contamination, assume well water is unsafe. Disinfect ("shock chlorinate") the well, pump it out, and boil/treat drinking water until it tests clean.
  • Rough shock-chlorination: add unscented bleach, mix through the well, let stand several hours, then pump until the chlorine smell is gone. (See a well guide for amounts by well size.)
  • When in doubt, BOIL well water for drinking and cooking.

Building a Safe Latrine

If toilets don’t work, build a simple pit latrine immediately — open defecation spreads disease fast.

Simple Pit Latrine

  • Dig a pit about 1 m (3 ft) wide and at least 2–3 m (6–10 ft) deep if you can.
  • Site it at least 30 m (100 ft) from and downhill of any water source, and away from where you live/cook.
  • Keep the pit bottom well above the groundwater table.
  • Cover with a slab or logs and soil, leaving a drop hole with a tight-fitting lid to keep flies out.
  • After each use, drop in a scoop of soil, ash, or lime to cut smell and flies.
  • Provide a way to wash hands nearby (see below). Keep it private, with a roof/walls if possible.
  • When the pit fills to about 0.5 m (1.5 ft) from the top, cover it completely with soil and dig a new one.

Cat-Hole (one person, short term)

Dig a hole 15–20 cm (6–8 in) deep, at least 60 m (200 ft) from water; cover with soil after use.

Handwashing — the cheapest medicine

Wash hands with soap and clean water after using the latrine, after handling waste or the sick, and before preparing or eating food. No running water? Use a "tippy-tap": a hung jug or bottle tipped to pour a thin stream while you scrub with soap or ash. This one habit prevents a huge share of outbreaks.

Keeping Sickness from Spreading

  • Bury or burn garbage; don’t let it pile up and breed flies and rats.
  • Keep flies off food and off anyone who is sick — cover food, cover the latrine hole.
  • Isolate people with diarrhea/vomiting; handle their waste carefully and wash thoroughly.
  • For diarrhea, the biggest danger is dehydration — give plenty of safe fluids and oral rehydration solution (1 liter clean water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt). See the medical guides.
  • Greywater (washing/cooking water) should soak away in a small soak-pit, not pool where people walk.
