🔫 Field dressing & gutting

Removing the guts quickly cools the meat and stops it spoiling. Do it as soon as the animal is dead — minutes matter, especially in warm weather.

⚠️ Work clean and protect yourself. Wear gloves if you have them. Keep the knife moving away from your other hand — a slip here, far from help, is dangerous. Don't cut into the guts, bladder or scent glands. Discard any animal whose organs look diseased (spots, swelling, foul smell, fluid).

Deer & other big game

  1. Position the animal on its back, head slightly uphill so fluids drain away from the chest.
  2. Ring the anus: cut a circle around the anus (and, on a female, the udder/genital opening) and free it so it can be pulled through later without spilling.
  3. Open the belly carefully. Pinch the skin up near the groin and make a small cut. Slide two fingers under the skin to lift the belly wall away from the guts, and run the knife up to the breastbone using your fingers as a guide — this keeps you from nicking the stomach and intestines.
  4. Free the innards. Reach in and pull the stomach and intestines out and to the side. Pull the freed anus/colon through from inside. Cut the diaphragm (the wall between guts and chest).
  5. Reach into the chest and cut the windpipe and gullet up high, then pull everything — lungs, heart, guts — out together. Roll it all out onto the ground.
  6. Save the good organs: heart, liver and kidneys are nutritious — set them aside in a clean bag (skip the liver if it looks spotted or abnormal).
  7. Drain and cool. Tip the carcass to drain blood, prop the chest open with a stick so air circulates, and get it into shade or cold air. Wipe the cavity clean — rinse with clean water only if you can then keep it dry.
💡 Cool is everything. Heat and trapped moisture spoil meat. Get the cavity open and airy, keep flies off (cloth game bag or pepper), and if it's cold out, let the carcass hang and cool before skinning and butchering.

Rabbits & small game

  1. Pinch up the belly skin and make a shallow cut from the breastbone to between the back legs, not cutting into the gut.
  2. Open the belly, hook the guts out with your fingers, and pull them free.
  3. Keep the heart, liver and kidneys. Many people then "shuck" a rabbit — a sharp downward shake can fling the guts clear.
  4. Cool and then skin it. Cook all small game thoroughly (see the disease note).

Birds (poultry & wild fowl)

  1. If keeping the skin, pluck first; otherwise skin the bird.
  2. Cut around and remove the vent (rear opening) without cutting the intestine.
  3. Make a small opening at the rear, reach in, and draw out the innards in one mass. Reach up to free the lungs against the back ribs.
  4. At the neck end, remove the crop (the food pouch) and windpipe.
  5. Save the heart, liver (remove the green gall bladder without bursting it) and the gizzard (split it, dump the grit, and peel the inner lining).
  6. Rinse, keep cold, and cook well.
Disease note Cook all wild meat thoroughly — no rare game. Wear gloves dressing rabbits and rodents (tularemia). Avoid animals that were found dead or acting sick.
