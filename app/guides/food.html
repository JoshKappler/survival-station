🌿 Food & Foraging

Finding, catching, growing, butchering and storing food — foraging, hunting, trapping, fishing and farming, with step-by-step guides plus every relevant book and manual on this computer.

⚠️ Read this first — wild food can kill. Never eat a wild plant or mushroom based on one source, a single picture, or the AI alone. Many deadly look-alikes exist, and some poisons act slowly. Cross-check every wild food in more than one guide, and when you are not 100% sure, don't eat it. Open the full safety note »

🍃 Foraging wild plants

Wild greens, roots, nuts and berries can keep you alive, but identification must be certain. Learn the few deadly families first (the carrot/parsley family with umbrella flower clusters, anything with milky sap, white or yellow berries), then build up a short list of plants you know cold.

  • Emergency Food & Poisonous Plants (TM 10-420)manual
  • Common Weeds of the United States (USDA, illustrated)manual
  • US Army Survival Manual — edible plants & the Universal Edibility Testmanual
  • Search everything for “edible wild plants”search

🍄 Mushrooms

Fungi are the highest-risk wild food. There is no general test that makes an unknown mushroom safe, and the Universal Edibility Test must never be used on fungi. Only eat mushrooms you can identify with total certainty against multiple guides.

  • Common Edible Mushrooms (illustrated)manual
  • Search everything for “poisonous mushrooms”search

🏹 Hunting & trapping

For calories that last, animal protein and fat are hard to beat. Trapping feeds you while you do other work — a dozen snares out overnight beat a day of stalking. Hunting takes a weapon, patience and knowing where animals feed and water.

🔫 Field dressing, gutting & skinning

Once an animal is down, the clock starts. Gut it quickly to cool the meat and avoid spoilage, keeping the guts intact so you don't taint the meat. Then skin it and get it cool and clean.

  • How-to: Field dressing & gutting (deer, small game, birds)guide
  • How-to: Skinning & pluckingguide
  • Search everything for “field dressing”search

🥩 Butchering & using the whole animal

Break the carcass into cuts you can cook and store, age it if it's cold enough, and waste nothing — render the fat into tallow or lard, boil bones for broth and save the hide. Fat is precious calories.

  • How-to: Butchering — breaking down a carcassguide
  • How-to: Rendering fat (tallow & lard) & whole-animal useguide
  • Cookery Vol. 3 — Meat, Poultry & Fish (cuts & cooking)book

🐟 Fishing

Fishing yields a lot of food for little energy, and you can fish passively with set lines, traps and nets while you do other things. Learn to clean and preserve a catch fast in warm weather.

🌱 Gardening & growing food

A garden is the difference between surviving a season and surviving for years. Favour calorie- and protein-dense crops (potatoes, beans, squash, corn), save your seeds, and learn your frost dates.

🐔 Raising animals (livestock & poultry)

Chickens, rabbits, goats and pigs turn scraps and forage into eggs, milk and meat year-round. Start with the easiest keepers (poultry and rabbits) and learn to spot and treat common illnesses.

🥫 Preserving & storing food

Food you can't store is food wasted. Drying, salting, smoking, canning and root-cellaring let a good harvest or a big animal feed you for months. Canning low-acid foods (meat, vegetables) safely needs a pressure canner — follow the USDA guide exactly.
