🥫 Preserving & storing food

A feast today is famine tomorrow unless you can store it. Drying, salting, smoking, canning and cellaring let one good hunt or harvest feed you for months.

⚠️ Bad preserving can poison you. Improperly canned low-acid food can grow botulism, which is deadly and you cannot see, smell or taste it. Follow the USDA canning guide on this computer exactly, use a pressure canner for meat and vegetables, and when a jar or batch seems off, do not taste it — destroy it.

Drying (the simplest, oldest method)

Remove the water and most things can't spoil. Works for meat (jerky), fish, fruit, and many vegetables.

  1. Slice thin — lean meat in thin strips with the grain, fruit in thin slices. Trim fat off meat (fat goes rancid).
  2. Salt meat/fish lightly first to draw moisture and discourage bacteria.
  3. Dry with warmth + airflow, not heat: hang in the sun and breeze, over (not in) a low fire, or in a warm dry room. Keep flies off with cloth or smoke.
  4. Done when meat is hard, dry and snaps — no soft or moist spots. Cool it fully before storing.
  5. Store dry and airtight, out of light. Check for mold; properly dried jerky keeps for months.

Salting & curing

Heavy salt preserves meat and fish without refrigeration. Dry cure: pack the meat completely in salt in a crock or box, in a cool place, and let it draw out the moisture. Brine cure: submerge in a strong salt solution (strong enough to float an egg). Rinse and soak before cooking to remove excess salt. (Curing salts with nitrites give the pink color and extra safety of ham/bacon, but plain salt preserves too.)

Smoking

  • Hot smoking (cooks and flavors): meat/fish held in warm smoke until cooked through — eat within days unless also dried/cured.
  • Cold smoking (preserves, doesn't cook): cool smoke over a long time after salting, to dry and flavor for longer storage. Needs care to stay cool and clean.
  • Use hardwoods (oak, hickory, fruitwoods). Avoid resinous softwoods like pine.

Canning (long storage in jars)

Sealing food in jars and heating it kills spoilers and creates a vacuum seal.

  • High-acid foods (most fruit, jams, pickles, tomatoes with added acid) can be safely processed in a boiling-water bath.
  • Low-acid foods (all meat, fish, beans and vegetables) must be processed in a pressure canner to reach the temperature that kills botulism spores. A boiling-water bath is not safe for these.
  • Follow the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning for exact times and pressures.

Root cellaring (no processing at all)

Many crops store for months if simply kept cold and humid (about 32–40°F) in a cellar, buried pit, or insulated box:

  • Keep well: potatoes (dark!), carrots, beets, turnips, parsnips, winter squash & pumpkins (these prefer cool + dry), onions & garlic (cool + dry), cabbages, apples.
  • Keep apples away from vegetables — apples give off a gas that makes others spoil.
  • Check often and remove anything starting to rot before it spreads.
💡 Pemmican & staples Combine dried pounded meat with rendered fat to make pemmican, which keeps for years. Dried beans, grains and rendered fat stored cool and dry are your long-term backbone.
