🧸 Skinning & plucking

Getting the hide or feathers off cleanly so the meat stays clean, cools fast, and the skin can be saved for later use.

💡 Easiest while warm. A hide pulls away most easily before the animal goes cold and stiff. Keep hair off the meat as you work, and keep your blade close to the skin, not the meat.

Big game (deer): hanging method

  1. Hang it by the hind legs from a sturdy branch or gambrel, head down, at a comfortable working height. Gravity does much of the work.
  2. Ring the legs: cut around each hind leg below the knee, then slit the inside of each leg up to the groin to meet your gutting cut.
  3. Start the hide off at the hind legs. Work your fist and knife between hide and meat, pulling down with your hands and only using the knife where the membrane holds. Pulling (not cutting) keeps the meat clean and the hair off.
  4. Work down the body, peeling the hide like a coat, until you reach the front legs and neck. Ring the front legs and free them.
  5. Finish at the neck. Cut the hide free at the neck, or sever the head to take hide and head together.
  6. Trim off hair, bloodshot meat and surface dirt. Now butcher it.

Rabbits & small game: case skinning

  1. After gutting, cut around both hind ankles and across the back of the hips between the legs.
  2. Work fingers under the skin at the hips and peel the hide toward the head like pulling off a sock — it comes off the body in one tube.
  3. Pull until you reach the front legs (pop them out) and the head; cut the skin free at the neck and wrists.
  4. Remove the feet and head, rinse, and keep the meat cool.

Birds: pluck or skin

Plucking (keeps the fatty, flavorful skin)

  • Dry plucking: as soon as the bird is dead and still warm, pull small pinches of feathers in the direction they grow so you don't tear the skin. Do the big wing and tail feathers first.
  • Scald plucking (faster): dunk the bird for a few seconds in water that is hot but not boiling (about 150°F / 65°C), then the feathers slip out easily. Don't overdo it or the skin tears.
  • Singe off the last hairs over a flame.

Skinning (faster, no scalding)

If you don't need the skin, simply slit the skin over the breast, peel it off the breast and legs, and remove wings — you lose some fat but it's quick.

Saving the hide

Don't waste a good hide. Scrape off all flesh and fat from the inside, then either dry it hard (rawhide for cordage, repairs, containers) or tan it for soft leather. The brains of most animals contain enough oil to tan their own hide.
