🐟 Fishing — lines, traps, nets & cleaning fish

Pound for pound, fish are some of the easiest wild protein to get — especially with passive methods that work while you sleep.

Where the fish are

  • Cover & edges: undercut banks, submerged logs, weed beds, rocks, and the edge between fast and slow water.
  • Depth by temperature: in heat, fish go deep or to shade; in cool weather they hold in shallower, sunlit water.
  • Best times: dawn and dusk, and after rain when food washes in.

Methods, easiest first

Set lines & trotlines (passive — best calorie return)

A baited hook on a line, tied off and left, fishes for you. A trotline is one long line stretched across the water with many baited hooks hanging from it. Bait with worms, insects, cut fish, guts, or dough. Check it a few times a day.

Hook & line

If you have hooks, fish actively with bait or a small lure. No hooks? Improvise a gorge hook: a short sliver of bone, wood or thorn sharpened at both ends, tied in the middle and buried in bait — the fish swallows it and it turns sideways to lodge in the throat.

Fish traps & weirs (great for streams)

Build a funnel of stones or stakes in a stream so fish swim in but can't easily find their way out, then trap or scoop them. A basket trap with a funnel mouth, baited and sunk, catches fish and crayfish overnight.

Nets & spears

A gill net set across a fish run is very effective where it's lawful and you have cordage to make one. Spearing works in clear shallows — aim below where the fish appears, because water bends light.

💡 Also easy: crayfish, frogs, shellfish. Don't ignore the small stuff. Crayfish under rocks, frogs at the water's edge, and freshwater mussels are easy protein. (Avoid shellfish that smell bad or come from fouled water.)

Cleaning a fish

  1. Scale (if it has scales): scrape from tail toward head with a knife back or spoon until smooth.
  2. Gut: slit the belly from the vent up to the gills, and pull out all the innards.
  3. Remove the gills (they spoil fast) and scrape out the dark blood line along the spine.
  4. Rinse in clean water. Cook whole, or fillet: cut down to the spine behind the head, then slide the knife along the backbone toward the tail to lift off each side.
⚠️ Fish spoil fast — and a few are toxic. Keep the catch cold or in water until cleaning, and eat or preserve it quickly in warm weather. Cook freshwater fish thoroughly (parasites). Don't eat fish that smell of ammonia or are slimy and dull-eyed. Avoid puffer-type fish and any local fish you know to be poisonous.

Keeping a catch

More than you can eat? Preserve it fast: split and salt, dry thin strips in sun and wind, or smoke it. See the preserving guide.
