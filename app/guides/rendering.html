🧊 Rendering fat & using the whole animal

Fat is concentrated calories — the thing you'll crave most on a lean wild diet. Rendering turns raw fat into clean, shelf-stable tallow or lard, and almost every other part of the animal has a use too.

Why fat matters so much Lean wild meat alone can't sustain you — without fat, you burn out ("rabbit starvation"). Rendered fat gives you cooking grease, candles, soap, waterproofing, and the binder for pemmican, the ultimate survival food. Never throw fat away.

Rendering fat into tallow (beef/deer) or lard (pig)

  1. Trim and chop the fat into small pieces (or grind it). The smaller the pieces, the faster and cleaner it renders. Cut away any meat or blood.
  2. Heat it low and slow. Put the fat in a heavy pot over low heat. A splash of water at the start stops scorching while things get going (it boils off). Stir now and then.
  3. Let it melt out. Over an hour or more the solid fat turns to clear liquid and the leftover bits ("cracklings") sink and turn golden. Keep the heat low — smoking or browning ruins the flavor and the keeping quality.
  4. Strain the liquid fat through cloth into clean jars or tins, leaving the cracklings behind.
  5. Cool and store. It sets into a firm white fat. Kept clean, dry and cool, rendered fat lasts for months to a year+ without refrigeration. The cracklings are a tasty, high-energy snack.
⚠️ Hot fat burns badly. Rendered fat gets very hot and sticks to skin. Keep water away from the hot pot (it spatters), work slowly, and keep children clear. Burns are a serious injury when help is far away.

Pemmican — fat + meat that keeps for years

  1. Dry lean meat hard and pound it to a powder (see Preserving food).
  2. Melt rendered tallow and mix it into the meat powder, roughly equal parts by weight, until it's like thick dough. Add dried berries if you have them.
  3. Press into cakes or pack into containers and let it set. Stored cool and dry, good pemmican keeps for months or years and is dense survival fuel.

Use every part

  • Bones: simmer for hours into broth/stock — nourishing, and it pulls out gelatin and minerals. Crack long bones for the rich marrow.
  • Organs: liver, heart, kidneys are nutrient-dense — cook thoroughly. (Don't eat the liver of predators/polar animals — too much vitamin A.)
  • Hide: dry hard for rawhide (cordage, soles, containers) or tan for leather. An animal's own brains usually contain enough oil to tan its hide.
  • Sinew: the tendons dry into superb cordage, thread and bowstring backing.
  • Hooves, skin & feet: long boiling yields gelatin and glue.
  • Stomach & bladder: cleaned, they make watertight containers.
  • Fat + wood ash + water makes lye soap — hygiene prevents disease.
