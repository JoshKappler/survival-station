🧾 Snares, deadfalls & trap lines

Traps work for you day and night while you do other things. A line of simple snares is one of the most reliable ways to feed yourself in the wild.

Why trap? One hunter can only be in one place. A dozen traps cover a dozen places at once, all night, using almost no energy. Trapping is usually a better calorie return than hunting when you are tired or hungry.

Read the ground: where to set

Animals are creatures of habit. Set your traps where they already travel — you are funneling them, not attracting them.

  • Game trails: worn paths through grass or brush, often with tracks and droppings.
  • Funnels & pinch points: gaps under fallen logs, holes in fences, narrow gaps between rocks or water and brush. Build little brush "fences" to steer animals into your snare.
  • Den and water entrances, feeding areas, and the edges of fields.

The simple wire snare

A snare is a loop that tightens as the animal pushes through. Use light snare wire if you have it; otherwise strong cordage or braided plant fiber.

  1. Make a small fixed loop (an "eye") at one end, then pass the other end through it so the wire slides freely — this forms a noose that only tightens.
  2. Size the loop to the animal's head: roughly a fist-size loop for rabbits, set so the bottom of the loop is about a hand's width off the ground.
  3. Anchor the free end solidly to a stake, root or heavy log the animal can't drag off.
  4. Support the loop open with a light twig or split stick so it stands in the trail like an open "O."
  5. Brush out your scent and disturbance; funnel the trail with sticks so the animal must put its head through the loop.
💡 The spring-pole upgrade. Tie the snare to a bent-over sapling held by a simple trigger. When the animal trips it, the sapling snaps up, lifting the catch off the ground — a faster kill, and out of reach of other scavengers.

Deadfalls

A deadfall drops a heavy rock or log onto the animal when it disturbs a baited trigger. The Paiute deadfall and the figure-4 are the classic triggers. Use a flat, heavy weight (several times the animal's weight) and bait the trigger with food the animal wants.

⚠️ Deadfalls and powerful snares can hurt you. A figure-4 under a heavy rock will smash fingers. Set the trigger with the rock propped on a safe support, keep your hands clear of the kill zone, and warn others where your sets are so no one (and no pet or child) stumbles into them.

Water sets

For animals that swim (muskrat, beaver, raccoon), a snare or trap set so the caught animal goes into deep water gives a quick, humane drowning set and keeps the catch from chewing free.

Running a trap line

  1. Set many. Most sets catch nothing on a given night. Ten to twenty simple snares give you real odds.
  2. Check daily, ideally at dawn. Reset sprung traps, re-bait, and move sets that never produce. Checking often is also the humane thing to do.
  3. Dispatch quickly with a sharp blow to the back of the head, then gut and skin the animal promptly.
  4. Keep notes in your head of which spots produce, and build your line around them.
Disease note Handle wild animals with care — wear gloves if you have them, and don't eat an animal that seemed sick or acted strangely. Rabbits in particular can carry tularemia; cook all wild meat thoroughly.
