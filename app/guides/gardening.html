🌱 Survival gardening & calorie crops

Foraging and hunting feed you for days; a garden feeds you for years. The goal is calories and protein you can grow, store, and re-plant from your own saved seed.

Grow calories, not just salad

Leafy greens are healthy but won't keep you alive. Prioritise crops that pack energy and store well:

  • Potatoes — the champion calorie crop; easy, productive, and they store in a cool cellar.
  • Beans & peas — protein, and dried beans store for years. Bush or pole.
  • Winter squash & pumpkins — heavy yields, store for months whole.
  • Corn (maize) — calories and grindable into meal; dries and stores well.
  • Root crops — beets, carrots, turnips, parsnips: store in the ground or a cellar.
  • Grains if you have land — wheat, oats, rye.
💡 The Three Sisters. Plant corn, climbing beans and squash together: the corn is a pole for the beans, the beans feed the soil with nitrogen, and the squash leaves shade out weeds and hold moisture. A proven low-input combo.

Soil and water are the whole game

  • Feed the soil: compost everything (kitchen scraps, leaves, manure, ash in moderation). Healthy soil = healthy crops with no store-bought fertiliser.
  • Mulch heavily with straw, leaves or grass clippings to hold water, block weeds and build soil.
  • Water deeply and less often to push roots down. In dry country, plant in slight basins and space plants wider. (See "Gardening Without Irrigation," below.)
  • Rotate crops — don't grow the same family in the same spot each year, to limit pests and disease.

Timing: know your frost dates

Most crops are killed by frost. Start cool-season crops (peas, greens, roots) early, plant tender crops (beans, squash, corn, tomatoes) only after the last spring frost, and harvest before the first fall frost. Use the planting calendar on this computer for what to plant when.

Save your own seed (so you can do it again)

  1. Grow open-pollinated / heirloom varieties — their seed comes back true. (Hybrid "F1" seed doesn't.)
  2. Let your best plants fully mature and go to seed — dry beans on the vine, let a few lettuces/greens bolt, save seed from the best fruits.
  3. Dry seed thoroughly, then store it cool, dark and dry in sealed containers. Label with the year.
  4. Keep more seed than you think you need, and don't eat your whole seed stock.
Protect the harvest Fence out deer and rabbits, pick bugs by hand, and encourage frogs and birds. Then store what you grow — see the preserving guide.
